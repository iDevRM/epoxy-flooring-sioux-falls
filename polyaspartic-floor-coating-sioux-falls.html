Polyaspartic Floor Coating in Sioux Falls, SD

Polyaspartic floor coating in Sioux Falls is the answer to the two problems epoxy has in South Dakota: it will not cure in a cold garage, and it takes most of a week before you can park on it. Polyaspartic is a polyurea-family resin that kicks in about two hours per coat, goes down in temperatures that stop epoxy cold, and holds its color under sun coming through an open door. We install it as a full system on residential garages and use it as the topcoat over flake and metallic floors on nearly every job we run.

What Does a Polyaspartic Floor Coating Involve?

It starts at the concrete, same as any coating worth paying for. We diamond grind the slab to open the pores, chase and fill cracks with a rigid filler, then vacuum the dust out with HEPA equipment so nothing sits in the bond line. No acid etching. Etching leaves residue behind and never profiles a floor evenly, which is why so many box-store kits peel off in sheets a year later.

Then the base coat goes down. If you chose a flake floor, colored vinyl chip gets broadcast into the wet resin to refusal, and once it sets we scrape and vacuum the excess back off. A clear polyaspartic topcoat finishes it. Because each coat sets in roughly two hours instead of overnight, the whole sequence fits inside one working day — usually 7am to mid-afternoon on a two-car garage.

One thing worth knowing before you book: polyaspartic runs on its own clock. Once the parts are mixed the crew is committed, and there is no pausing for lunch. That is why it is a poor DIY product and why crew size matters more than it does with epoxy.

When Do You Need Polyaspartic Instead of Epoxy?

Cold is the first and biggest reason in Sioux Falls. Standard 100% solids epoxy wants a slab around 55°F to cure to full hardness, and an unheated garage here spends November through March well under that. A coating installed too cold cures soft, stays tacky, or never hardens fully — then fails the next summer and the product gets the blame instead of the schedule.

Second is downtime. If the house is going on the market, or you only have one weekend, one day of lost garage access beats a week of it.

Third is sunlight. A west-facing garage door left open on a July afternoon will amber a standard clear epoxy within a couple of seasons. Polyaspartic does not yellow.

Fourth is abrasion and salt. It is a harder, more flexible film, so it takes hot tire contact, dropped tools, and winter brine with less damage.

If your garage is heated, the floor never sees direct sun, and you are not in a hurry, epoxy is fine and costs less. We will say so on site rather than upsell you.

Why Do Garage Floors Fail in South Dakota Winters?

Freeze-thaw is doing most of the damage. Sioux Falls crosses the freezing line dozens of times between November and March, and every cycle is a chance for water sitting in porous concrete to expand and pop the surface layer. That is what spalling is — the top eighth-inch of a slab flaking away in patches, usually worst right where the car parks.

The brine makes it faster. Road salt and ice melt drip off a vehicle onto the slab, soak into unsealed concrete, and stay there. Chloride attacks the cement paste that holds the aggregate together, so a floor that was solid five winters ago starts shedding sand when you sweep it.

The third failure is one nobody tests for: moisture pushing up from below. Plenty of Sioux Falls slabs were poured with a thin or missing vapor barrier, and water vapor migrating up gets trapped under a coating that cannot breathe. We run a calcium chloride or in-slab humidity test before quoting, and if the numbers are high a vapor-barrier primer goes down first. That adds about $1.00 to $1.75 per square foot and it is the difference between a floor that lasts fifteen years and one that blisters in the second spring.

What Affects the Cost of Polyaspartic Coating in Sioux Falls?

Budget $5 to $9 per square foot installed. A 480 square foot two-car garage generally lands between $2,400 and $4,200. National pricing for the same system runs $5 to $12 per square foot, with two-car garages quoted as high as $6,000 in higher-cost metros.

What moves the number:

System build. A single-color two-coat floor sits at the low end. A full flake broadcast with a double clear adds roughly $1 to $2 per square foot.

Prep depth. Grinding off an old failed coating adds $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot before any new material goes down.

Moisture mitigation. $1.00 to $1.75 per square foot when the slab tests high.

Crew size and season. Large floors need more hands to beat the cure clock, and a deep-winter install may need heaters staged in the space. Both are real cost, not markup.

Edges and obstacles. Steps, floor drains, and built-in cabinets slow the pour and add hand-cut detail work.

Every quote is written and itemized before work starts, with the moisture reading on it. If the slab tests dry, that line comes off the price.

Polyaspartic vs. Epoxy: Which Do You Need?

Epoxy goes on thicker per coat, costs less, and is better at filling minor surface imperfections. It needs about 24 hours between coats, a week before vehicles, a slab above 55°F, and it will amber wherever sun reaches it. Good for heated basements, storage rooms, and interior spaces with no daylight.

Polyaspartic costs 30 to 40 percent more, cures in hours, handles cold and UV, and resists salt better. Better for garages, anything with sun exposure, winter scheduling, and any job where downtime is expensive.

Most of our Sioux Falls garage jobs end up as a hybrid: an epoxy base coat for build and fill, then a polyaspartic clear on top for cure speed, UV, and abrasion. You get the strengths of both and it costs less than an all-polyaspartic system. Anyone quoting you a pure system without asking about your heat, your sun exposure, and your timeline is selling whatever is already on the truck.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you coat my garage floor in the winter in Sioux Falls?

Yes, and that is the main reason to choose polyaspartic here. Standard epoxy needs the slab around 55°F to cure properly, which rules out most unheated Sioux Falls garages from November through March. Polyaspartic cures far colder, so winter installs are a normal job rather than a gamble.

How much does polyaspartic floor coating cost in Sioux Falls, SD?

Polyaspartic runs $5 to $9 per square foot installed in Sioux Falls, so a 480 square foot two-car garage lands around $2,400 to $4,200. That is roughly 30 to 40 percent above a straight epoxy build, and it buys a one-day install, better salt resistance, and no yellowing.

How fast can I park on a polyaspartic floor?

Walk on it 4 to 6 hours after the last coat and park on it after 24 hours. A standard two-car garage in Sioux Falls is ground, coated, and finished in one working day, so you lose a single day of garage access instead of the week a full epoxy build needs before it takes tire weight.

Does polyaspartic hold up to road salt and ice melt?

Better than epoxy. Polyaspartic is more chemically resistant and more flexible, so brine dripping off a vehicle sits on the surface and mops up instead of soaking into the slab. It also flexes with the concrete through the freeze-thaw swings that pit uncoated Sioux Falls garage floors.
