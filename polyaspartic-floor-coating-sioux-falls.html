Polyaspartic Floor Coating in Sioux Falls, SD

Ask three contractors in Sioux Falls to coat your garage in February and two will tell you to wait until May. That is not caution, it is the material talking — conventional resin stops behaving in the cold. Polyaspartic does not have that limitation, and it happens to be tougher against the brine your tires drag in from Minnesota Avenue. This page covers the winter problem first, then walks you through an install hour by hour, then shows you how to read a quote so you can tell two bids apart.

The Cold-Weather Problem Nobody Quotes For

Resin chemistry is temperature-dependent in a way paint is not. Conventional two-part epoxy wants the concrete itself near 55°F to reach full hardness. Below that the reaction slows, then stalls. What you get is a floor that feels dry to the touch and never actually finishes hardening — it dents under a jack stand, hazes under a hot tire, and fails the following summer, at which point everyone blames the brand.

An attached garage in Sioux Falls sits somewhere in the forties for months, and a detached one is worse. Concrete also lags air temperature by a day or more, so a warm afternoon in March does not mean a warm slab. Contractors who work year-round either heat the space for days beforehand or switch materials.

Polyaspartic keeps reacting far below the point where epoxy quits. We still stage heaters to hold conditions steady and we still check slab temperature with an infrared gun before mixing, but the calendar stops being the constraint. Roughly a third of our garage work happens between Thanksgiving and the thaw for exactly this reason.

What a One-Day Install Actually Looks Like

People hear “one day” and assume corners are being cut. Here is the real sequence on a two-car garage.

Morning, first two hours. Everything comes out of the garage. We run a diamond grinder across the whole slab to open the surface and knock off any old sealer, chasing the perimeter with a hand machine. Dust goes into a HEPA vacuum, not into the air.

Next hour. Cracks get opened up and filled, control joints get treated, and any pitted areas are patched flush. We take the moisture reading now if we have not already.

Late morning. Base coat goes down. If you picked a flake floor, colored chip is broadcast into the wet resin until the surface will not take any more.

Early afternoon. The base has set enough to walk on. Loose flake gets scraped and vacuumed back off, which is the messiest and most tedious part of the day.

Mid afternoon. Clear topcoat, then we are out.

You can walk on it that evening. Vehicles go back the next day. Compare that with a full epoxy build, which needs overnight between coats and about a week before it will take tire weight without marking.

What Road Salt Does to an Uncoated Slab

Bare concrete looks solid and behaves like a sponge. Chloride brine drips off the underside of a vehicle, wicks into the pores, and goes to work on the cement paste binding the aggregate together. The visible result arrives in stages: first a chalky film when you sweep, then shallow pits around the parking spots, then genuine spalling where the top layer flakes off in sheets.

Freeze-thaw accelerates every stage of it. Sioux Falls crosses the freezing line dozens of times each winter, and water sitting in those pores expands each time it turns. Salt makes it worse twice over, because brine holds liquid water at temperatures where plain water would already be frozen solid.

A sealed floor interrupts the whole sequence at step one. Nothing soaks in, so nothing can freeze inside the slab or attack the paste. Puddles of melt water sit on the surface until you push them out the door with a squeegee. That is the entire maintenance routine.

How to Read a Polyaspartic Quote

Two bids on the same garage can differ by well over a thousand dollars and both can be honest. Here is where the money actually goes.

Surface preparation method. Diamond grinding or shot blasting are the only acceptable answers. If a bid says acid etch or does not mention prep at all, that is the difference in price and it is also the reason that floor will peel.

Film thickness. Ask for total dry mils. A serious residential build lands around 20 to 30. Thin systems can be sprayed on fast and look identical for about eighteen months.

Moisture testing. It should be a line item with a number written on it, not a verbal assurance. Mitigation primer, if needed, runs about $1.00 to $1.75 per square foot.

Warranty language. Materials-only warranties are close to worthless, since the material almost never fails. Adhesion is what fails. Find out which one you are buying.

Local pricing lands between $5 and $9 per square foot installed, so a 480 square foot two-car garage runs roughly $2,400 to $4,200. Nationally the same work is quoted at $5 to $12, with some metros hitting $6,000 on a two-car space.

When Epoxy Is Still the Better Buy

We install both, and we will talk you out of the more expensive one when it does not earn its keep.

Epoxy makes more sense in a heated basement or utility room where nothing sees daylight, the temperature is stable, and you are not in a hurry to use the space. It goes on thicker per coat, so it does a better job of filling a rough or slightly uneven slab, and it costs meaningfully less.

Polyaspartic earns the premium when the space is cold, sunlit, on a deadline, or under vehicles carrying winter chemicals. In Sioux Falls that describes most garages, which is why most of our garage quotes lean that direction.

The build we actually recommend most often is neither one alone: an epoxy body coat for thickness and fill, wearing a polyaspartic clear on top for cure speed, color stability, and abrasion. It costs less than an all-polyaspartic system and outperforms a pure epoxy one. If a salesperson pushes a single chemistry without asking whether your garage is heated or which way the door faces, they are selling inventory rather than solving your problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is January a bad time to have a garage floor done in Sioux Falls?

Not with this material. Winter is our steadiest season for it, because the resin keeps reacting at slab temperatures that leave conventional epoxy soft and sticky. We still bring heaters to hold the concrete steady during the pour, but the calendar stops being the thing that decides your project date.

What should I look for on a polyaspartic quote?

Four things: grinding rather than acid etching, a written moisture reading, total dry film thickness of at least 20 mils, and whether the warranty covers adhesion or just materials. Two bids can differ by $1,200 purely because one of them skipped mil thickness, and you cannot see that difference until year three.

Why does my neighbor's garage floor look yellow?

Sunlight. Clear epoxy ambers when ultraviolet light reaches it, and a south or west facing overhead door left open on summer afternoons is enough to do it in two or three seasons. Polyaspartic is built on a different chemistry that holds its color, which is why we use it as the wear layer even over an epoxy base.

Will ice melt eat through the coating?

No. Chloride brine attacks bare cement paste, which is why uncoated slabs here start shedding sand after a few winters. A sealed floor keeps the brine on the surface where a mop removes it. Rinse the floor a few times over winter and the coating will outlive the vehicle parked on it.
